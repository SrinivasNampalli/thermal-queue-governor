## Model and observation

Four states represent drive current (A), winding temperature (°C), housing temperature (°C), and sensor temperature (°C). A first-order current regulator tracks a normalized command multiplied by rated current and a declared load factor. Current is integrated analytically. Temperature-dependent copper resistance produces `I²R` heat. Separate winding/housing heat capacities and winding→housing→ambient thermal conductances produce thermal storage and lag. The sensor has its own first-order lag before bounded noise and clipping at 95°C.

Thermal and sensor states use RK4 with a requested maximum step of 25 ms; commands and observations update every second. The integrator further limits steps to current time constant/8, sensor time constant/4, and bounds based on winding/housing thermal rates. It reports the actual step and substep count, and rejects advances requiring more than one million substeps. The held-out output would report the maximum winding temperature resolved across those substeps, separately from the next one-second endpoint. This detects resolved intersample excursions but is **not a proof of the continuous-time maximum**. The three proposed refinement cases halve both the requested and physics-derived step limits and compare peaks, endpoint histories and commanded-delivery fractions against the protocol tolerances.

The governor sees the noisy/clipped/missing sensor packet, exact accepted eight-slot FIFO, requested effort, and reported applied command. It does not see current, housing temperature, winding truth, future disturbances, or held-out parameter values. Its commissioning interval is preset from the scenario configuration; the simulator only draws the actual initial winding temperature within that interval. The hot interval upper end is 97.5°C, below both 98°C and 105°C operating ceilings. Such commissioning knowledge is assumed, not a sensor-derived physical procedure.

## Calibration, then held-out execution

The draft proposes seeds 4100–4102 with two 600-second open-loop profiles, for six plant executions. The excitation profile changes among command levels every 40 seconds. The hot-cooling profile starts at winding/housing temperatures of 100/55°C, with zero command for 120 seconds, full command for 240 seconds, then zero for 240 seconds. These numerical choices require review against the selected motor; this generic study is not being run.

Scalar cooling coefficients come from the frozen winding conductance/capacitance ranges. The lower heating coefficient is zero; the upper coefficient uses the frozen current, resistance, capacity and 160°C temperature guard. The additive upper allowance is the maximum positive one-step residual across calibration data plus 0.05°C/sample. The observation-error allowance is the largest calibration sensor-to-winding difference plus the 0.15°C noise bound and 0.15°C padding. These empirical allowances are calculated once, **not fitted or enlarged using held-out results**. Calibration success is not a global hidden-state enclosure certificate. Model construction failure is reported rather than repaired by changing the protocol.

The draft held-out seeds 5100–5102 are disjoint. Each plant/noise/demand realization would be paired across the existing `interval_queue` TQG and `fixed_cap` policy and across ceilings of 105°C and 98°C. The proposed design contains **72 primary held-out episodes**, plus three separately counted numerical-refinement reruns; none has run. Calibration, primary episodes, and refinement reruns must not be summed into the historical 1,120-episode claim.

## Challenges and outcomes

Normal operation begins at 65°C winding temperature. The clipped scenario starts near 96°C; the remaining challenge scenarios use this same hot start. Dropout suppresses readings during seconds 200–259. An ambient change raises ambient from 25°C to 43°C at second 200. A load change multiplies commanded current by 1.35 during seconds 200–449. The acknowledgment fault applies full drive command during seconds 200–419 while continuing to report the queued command. Those three changes are explicit tests beyond the calibration conditions.

Even without a fault, drive command and actual RMS current differ because the current loop retains energy after a zero command. Record commanded, reported and actual RMS effort separately. Delivered-command fraction measures normalized drive commands executed divided by their associated requested commands; it is not torque, shaft work, energy efficiency, or physical productivity. Fault-induced extra current must not be credited as useful commanded delivery.

Report operating-ceiling breaches and physical-105°C breaches separately, both at one-second endpoints and resolved substeps. Also report interval misses, invalid-controller samples, sensor-error-envelope misses, clipped/missing observations, minimum physical temperature margin, and elapsed-time throughput. Do not interpret an invalid controller returning zero as canceling previously accepted FIFO commands or current-loop tail heat. Publish failures and paired results; do not tune until green.

## Physics provenance

The model structure is motivated by ordinary current-squared heating, thermal storage and separate thermal paths, as discussed in [maxon motor simulation guidance](https://support.maxongroup.com/hc/en-us/articles/360013761160-Motor-data-and-simulation), [motor heating](https://support.maxongroup.com/hc/en-us/articles/360004427413-On-the-heating-of-motors-in-hand-held-tools), and [I²t protection](https://support.maxongroup.com/hc/en-us/articles/4412763373970-maxon-controllers-I2t-Limitation-Motor-winding-protection). Those references **do not supply or validate our numerical parameters**. This averaged current model omits PWM switching, electrical phase waveforms, back EMF, rotor speed, torque, magnetic saturation and detailed cooling geometry.
