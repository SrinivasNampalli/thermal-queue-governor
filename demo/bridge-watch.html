Read the walkthrough transcript
  1. Look inside the motorOrbit the assembly and separate the parts. The motor winding, attachment bond, sensing pad, heat shunt, thermal guard, and acquisition electronics can each be inspected.
  2. Two independent thermal controlsThe shunt cools the sensing pad into its readable range. A separate heater/cooler changes the temperature of the guard around the sensor leads. Its actuator and thermometer are shown as functional components.
  3. Inspect the measurement electronicsThe ADC reads the pad. The reference and guard thermometers provide the boundary temperatures. The estimator uses these observations and the assumed shunt calibration. The illustrated circuit is not electrically simulated.
  4. Recover information after clippingThe hypothetical direct winding channel stays at its 95°C ceiling. Cooling the pad makes the pad measurement readable. Three settled observations fit the thermal model; a fourth observation checks its consistency. In the synthetic nominal case, the estimate is about 130°C.
  5. Why the separate guard mattersWith strong heat leakage through the wires, a shunt-only model that ignores that path returns 85°C. In the constructed guarded case, the independent guard-temperature change allows the fit to recover the 130°C source.
  6. A pass can still be wrongAn extra heat path that bypasses the guard causes the model to report 85°C for a 130°C source. The fourth-state check still passes. Consistency is not an accuracy guarantee or a safety bound.
  7. Continue exploringThe interactive page contains 18 clickable components and 10 synthetic cases, including failure cases. The geometry is conceptual; no physical prototype has been built or validated by this recording.
